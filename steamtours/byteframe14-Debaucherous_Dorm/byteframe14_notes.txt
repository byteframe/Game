================================================================================ [h1]ASSET ACCUMLATION[/h1]
This project started, as they all do, with the accumulation of an asset pile. I discovered the existence of the 'The SceneBuild Megapack', as well as some other stuff from the Alyx workshop and (at that moment) was able to sense the months of work ahead of me to subsume and mold it into something. I return to that moment now, finally having pushed through.

The shipping graphical presentation overall is upgraded, but still a bit inconsistent. A starting intent was to avoid overuse of source1 assets, yet they remain a valuable resource for second order things like decals and sounds.

I wrote serviceable script(let)s that converts content from Half-Life Alyx into SteamVR. The engine can load the compiled models from the newer game, but cannot recompile them, so the vmdl_c's were extracted unmodified; the transformation of the materials is the primary work here. I did end up just shunting everything to vr_standard, inverting roughness maps/values, and (critically) assigning the metal texture to metalness AND reflectance. The vr_simple shader in SteamVR was not as capable than the Alyx variant (no normals or ao in the former) so those were put to vr_standard as well. Overall, it was mostly a arduous text processing affair. Obviously, alot of stuff doesn't work; xen and bottle shaders, as well other effects related to lighting.

The 'SceneBuildMegaPack' has been around for a while, and I found the original Source 1 version (which was easier to port to SteamVR by way of source1import) and that constitutes the bulk. The remaining assets that were added were from the Alyx variant, and I applied these on top. The author of the S2FM version did not include physics so that portion did not have any provided collision, which would become a problem later.

PBR downloaders and the material script were improved. Texturecan and Polyhaven (where) new additions. I also switched from 2k to 4k on ambientcg. The polyhaven texture downloader was later corrected to gather the metalness file and to apply other fixes, and it soon pbr.sh gained support for single one-off invocations that create pbr materials from urls -- in it's normal mode, the script still scours a content addon for zips and unmade materials to process.

As big of a lift as anything was to port the library of gorgeous models at polyhaven to SteamVR. The existing material downloader didn't require too much work to support the URLS for models and the associated textures. I attempted to just wrap the downloaded FBX file in a VMDL stub, but that only worked for very small number of them. In almost all cases, the material information baked into the model would not suffice unmodified, and everything was all covered in that red shit. Ergo, I had to 'wash' each fbx through the model editor import dialog, as one does. I wonder (now) if I could have gotten away with the fbx vmdl stubs and then patching them with material remaps, but this was very long ago, and I also didn't know about that trick at the time. Love me some material remaps these days, though.

At some point it occurred to me that non-power of two textures aren't the end of the world, and that I could write a simple script to wrap around arbitrary picture files/urls. So... I ripped a couple magazine cover websites and had a *bountiful* harvest of cool/creepy posters and german magazines to play with! This seemed hedonistic, and I felt glutinous. After I was 'done', I deleted my collection of these assets, figuring I could just get them JIT from URL's. The CoverJunkie website is now defunct, and I lost this resource going forward. Seemed like fate. [spoiler]In any event I have developed a fear that these bogus jpg dimensions are giving the texture streamer hassles, and will be more strict in the next project.[/spoiler]

================================================================================ [h1]ORGANISATION[/h1]
Prior to byteframe13, I didn't group anything or use selection sets and the outliner was a big soup just like the map. I went hard on selection sets in byteframe13, but for anything other than temporary holding bags, they are slow and sucky and somewhat dangerous (re crashing). I now see it as best to get OCD with actual object groups and a carefully arranged outliner. This is the case for any 3d program ofc, but it's just not obvious here because the outliner UI in Hammer doesn't let your reorder anything. [spoiler]To move things you basically have to delete them and hit ctrl-z and then they're at the bottom of whatever part of the tree they were in before.[/spoiler]

Groups are faster and more 'real', but they present the issue of a new pivot point to fret about. Getting the proper pivot for a prefab is definitely worthwhile, but for groups it can often times be skippable. There were some small wins here and there nonetheless, as mirroring the groups was made easier, and some fuck ups were fixed in a jiff. There is piece of mind inherent to having a big chunk of work locked in it's own container/file, and set to a power-of-two grid coordinate, with transforms locked. Having to sort everything in a group also helped me sport errant objects and junk I forgot to delete.

I went hard in the paint using prefabs for tons of things. [i]Incidentally, instances are buggy with lighting entities and perhaps more.[/i] I also figured out what the hell 'map variables' were (finally), and so any opportunity was taken to give a prefab some kind of flag or knob to fiddle.

Yet, nothing is ever perfect. With 20 dorms, each having tons of silly variables and complex prefabs nesting... it wasn't long before Hammer was screaming for mercy. I'm talkin' like, 80 second long pauses when switching a prefab or even looking at my monitor sideways. I started removing as many variables as possible and converting tons of logic setups to vscript, and made other simplifications and reductions. Blah blah. It took some time, but the editor went back to being cooperative, and the dorms are better for it in regardless to some prop-placement concerns.

================================================================================ [h1]GEOMETRY AND TEXTURING[/h1]
Hard to say if I advanced in geometry construction by acquiring a large amount of new skills and patterns. I'm sure I gathered a bit, but I was Bob the Builder at the end of byteframe13, and this environment is all terrestrial -- so while the level geometry was made quick and perfect, it's all quite flat. The level I mean. The actual features look sexy, but tl:dr, I was hoping to have a big atrium in the middle with stairs and verticality. There wudn't enough space. The main advance I guess was to coordinate topology with a desired texturing method at the outset. I was more judicious in employing use of the (not to innocent it seems) skip texture to allow for more easy and re-repeatable room peels. Having to go back in time and try to re-texture a room (guessing peel values from memory ecause I fudged a nudge) is always very painful, particularly with brick and wood. Therefore, the scaffolding of the skip textures and a note somewhere helped me band-aid these quickly. Luckily, one can avoid most of this by just making sure the third texture lock option is on 24/7.

Christ though, if I had learned about the god-damn [b][u]'Wrap Texture'[/u][/b] functions earlier! It can fix so much stuff with just a tap of finger, but then later becomes an important part of texturing schemes overall. All those little dingle berries at a feature's extremities can be be fixed with this, which also means that geometry doesn't have to be staged with _such_ an extreme adherence to the pre-planned peel. The 'Wrap All To Texture' can be used to pull off some stuff that I though wasn't really possible before. [spoiler]The particular keyboard combination it uses (combined with my speed) made me feel like I'm operating the level editor program similarly to a musical instrument. 'Wrap All to Texture is 'D' on the trumpet.[/spoiler]

================================================================================ [h1]LIGHTING[/h1]
As per usual, my lighting skills only grew a little bit. The main advanced was a workaround for a bug; in SteamTours (dunno about HLVR) a prop_static will not respect the 'no shadows' flag in a vrad3 build of the map. Turns out, one should just set a light/lamp model to be a prop_dynamic instead of converting it to a mesh (as I was doing before). The best solution might be to turn off shadows in the model's material(s) and then go back to prop_static, but iirc, I think I noticed something at the end that dissuaded me from trusting this.

As an aside, the kissing cousin of this bug remains unfixed: [u]I still don't know why overlays don't show up on static props in vrad3 builds![/u] Going forward, I certainly won't be converting models just for the sake of an overlay, and just avoid mixing these things. Of course, if the target surface is flat, then I can decide to maybe convert the overlay to a quad instead.

[u]I did my darnest to understand the light attenuation stuff.[/u] I kinda understand it now, or at least have decided on how I will fiddle the knobs. I keep quadratic at the default, and just change the linear attenuation to deal with the super high brightness splotches that arise in certain situations where the light is really close to surfaces relevant to the player. I leave the falloff variable and light source radius variables alone, because I'm still scared of them. I have always been timid to dive into some of the advanced lighting because I assume most of it only shows up after a bake, and I mostly can just peruse source 1 tutorials about this kinda stuff -- but I sense I will continue to make strides with future projects.

I decided, come what may, that I was going to ship those corny cubemap bathroom mirrors you oftentimes see in games. [spoiler]In Condition Zero there is this insanely charming hand drawn one).[/spoiler] Turns out if you really stretch the bounds, you can get it to looks ostensibly passable. The distance and bounds values are hard to get right and to iterate on. They can only be made so big before things get complicated RE overlap and priority, but what I have is nice. Still corny, but I liked them. [spoiler]They probably could have been blurred more for effect, or maybe a future shower prefab can have them fog up when the hot water turns on! I bet the material_changer entities don't function though.[/spoiler]

I really (really) tried to get Baked Light Indexing to work. This is an HLVR feature that might be functional in SteamTours, but is unexposed until one puts the right stuff into gameinfo and the FGD... and I was unable to do that. I tried it every which way, either I missed some other step, or it just doesn't work, and I suspect the latter. I'm used to making the hard choices about which lights to bake, but was only really considering the loss of specular (because of prior submissions' large use of static props). Just about everything in byteframe14 is a physics prop, and I kinda didn't fully realize these won't cast shadows in the baked zones -- of which there aren't that many. I think I eeked by overall here, with just two dim dead-end areas of the map suffering. [b]With Baked Light Indexing I could (afaict) get everything to cooperate.[/b] In any event, vrad and vviz both have an easy time building byteframe14. A standard 8k build is done in a single sleep with 6 cores! The final build takes 21. [spoiler]I imagine one day I might look back on this and wish more stuff was static and covered in luxels, and will guess that It wasn't the style at the time.[/spoiler]

I finally gave into vrad's bitching, and threw in a lightmap player space volume. I was always reticent to do this in my maps because just about every surface is close enough or relevant to the player that I didn't think it mattered, and would just find some low hanging fruit to down-bias, calling it a day. I quess I do both now.

================================================================================ [h1]CUSTOM CUBEMAPS[/h1]
At some point I finally figured out how to do custom cubemaps (there's a button for it!) and tried my best to use this to my advantage to save memory. In addition to a simple "in the void" cube for the airlocks (saving 3 8MB textures), the four colored hallways each [i]had[/i] three volumes sharing one cubemap for each color (saving 8 textures).

The visual hit from using one cubemap texture for three boxes wasn't too bad, but I encountered a [b]maddening[/b] issue that plagued me throughout the project: when the player is close enough to the edges of two hallway cubes, they can cock their head/mouse to either side and the one of the cubes...does....something. [i]It would flicker on and off as you moved...or maybe the objects were toggling between which cube to use?[/i] I got a real good look at the reflections near the end and it indeed seemed like the texture itself was flipping upside down, which seemed to track after fiddling with the priority levels, like, once a week and always coming up short. 

I fought for it up until the end, and it probably wouldn't of been a deal breaker if I _had_ to ship the busted hallway cubes, but it's reproduciblilty was too 'eclectic', and I retreated. I eventually bit the bullet and had to use one for each box in the hallways, like one normally does. The reflections on the railings are sexier for it, I suppose. [spoiler]I sure hope I can figure out how to go crazy with custom cubemaps all adjacent to one another, otherwise the testing floor hallways might be considerably less feasible.[/spoiler]

================================================================================ [h1] DORM LIGHT TOGGLE[/h1]
I also used shared custom cubemaps (successfully this time) in the dormrooms, one for when the light was on, and a small real dark one for when it was off. I was never really sure how much of this effect/system I was going to get to work, and had a more 'poke at it every now and then' strategy until it cohered. After finishing the dorm vizboz system (which handles the lights as well) I was still left with each room still getting it's own indirect lightmap, thereby making the room glow when the light was off. [u]It took a while to get the mesh (later model) to not receive baked lighting information.[/u] After trial and error (and bug fixing with the cube switching io/code), checking vpk contents, etc, and realizing that the glow could be from the hallway cube -- during a total vizbox shutdown whereby both dormcubes were disabled. I sorted it out. The dormrooms, and their contents are all dynamic objects without lightmap information, and I was also able to apply this to the outside areas too, increasingly luxel density through the rest of the map! [u]A main takeway, I believe one has to change more than just the Baked Lighting flag to ensure no bake.[/u]

The two dormroom volumes also employed their own custom light probe textures. My goal was to avoid the needless generation of 20 identical ones on build, and to also darken one of them for the off-volume. It was very awkward to arrange all the steps for this, but it did seem to have some kind of effect, in a vrad 2 build at least. Examining the compiled textures in vrad3 didnt show any difference, and it seemed like the darker cubemap texture (which was respected in both builds) does most of the work anyway, so I succumbed to the normal method and removed the custom light probe textures.

During my travels I was also able to finally get Light Groups to do something. With vscript you can, I guess programatically set objects to use one cube or another at runtime...? I gave it a go, but it was deficient in some crude/basic way as applied to the dorm toggle problem. Lil bit a research.

As you can imagine, there was tons of pain in getting every thing to spawn in the right place. I _probably_ should have just manually filled in all the dressers and desks and so on by hand, (it'd of been done in a relative heartbeat), instead of getting feisty with an underdeveloped lua/vscript skillset. It even culls some of the triangles of a big room mesh. [spoiler]There's something I'm not getting about custom entity attributes.[/spoiler]

I also made the script not precache any models that were already in the map, though, I imagine this is pointless.

================================================================================ [h1]PHYSICS WOES[/h1]
This is the swamp I got mired in.

With polyhaven_models imported (which provided actual vmdls unlike source1 content), I found myself creating collision models in ModelEditor for the first time! [i]This information was present in the wiki, but I stumbled upon it myself, so it was fun... at first.[/i] Ugg: I gather that creating collision models is frustrating skut work nobody in gamedev likes doing, so in many cases there is a 'just spit something out and move on' aspect to the work. To that end I can't say I get too technical fiddling with the knobs in Convex Decomposition, but I continue to strive. If it was required, I got at as granular as needed to support proper physics and interactions. There's only so far I can go considering modeleditor's capabilities (and bugs), and this included being forced to edit the vertices by hand in some extreme instances.

For physics props, I could get always something workable in a few clicks with ModelEditor, but for more complex static props that can 'contain' or have props rest upon them (cabinets, even tables), I typically have to create companion mesh(es) in the map and texture it with tools/invisible. I tried to add this back into the model, but it didn't seem to work and it had become too late in the project to overcome this deficiency in my process. I would have had to do this less (and rely just on easy mesh collision) but thin physics props like magazines would fall through all but the simplest edges of these larger structures. A tabletop with a curved smooth or curved edges would be problematic, for instance (the object would only be kosher near the middle of the surface). Singular convex hulls are resistant to this error, but it is cumbersome and annoying to have to split maintain groups of collision meshes or scrub a model. I wonder what a better solution could be.

Initially, however, I figured I could just wrap all the physics-less models that I wanted to be pickup-able in func_physbox mesh entities (and not bother with ModelEditor or conversions at all). Indeed, testing in 2D window showed this to be a workable solution, and so I implemented this for ALL the afflicted props. Hundreds or so physboxes. All the magazines and frames and cartridges etc, etc. Took days. PeeBee's as far as the eye could see. It seemed gangily, but workable...

PLOT TWIST! Turns out picking physbox-wrapped junk up in VR Mode doesn't work! Once the user picks up the physbox it (in just about all instances) succumbs to some BOGUS orientations and position relative to the hand. The 'inherit carry angles' stuff didn't seem to help, or do anything. If setting those flags correctly COULD fix the problem... then wtf is happening? [u][b]When you pick up a physbox with your hand in SteamVR, does it think I'm using the half life 2 gravity gun?![/b][/u] That is still my working theory, and still seems like a cool guess... but I didn't really delve deeper or ask around. (Maybe having the angles of the model and the box be all zeroed out prior to user pickup would make them more cooperative, didn't really press on that either). It seemed best to just groan, steel myself... and get goin' washing a fuck ton of models. A week of solid non-stop clicking (on top of the initial botched physbox campaign)......ugggggggh! It felt like a month, and still has me sour.

Procedure/workflows for washing/reconverting the models (such that I could THEN add the physics with Model Editor) were varied. My first instinct was to just use Hammer to convert it to a mesh, and then convert THAT into a model. Some complex models (computer keyboards) might be too detailed for this method and so bad faces would show up. [spoiler]Though I suppose I could just have scaled them larger before converting to mesh.[/spoiler]. Later I would start going though a painful routine involving Blender Source Tools. I figured this would also benefit me by getting at original source quads instead of the Hammer triangulation, but I shouldn't have expected that to work! [i]Source 1 models (at least as processed by Crowbar) are still going to be triangle-y.[/i] Quads are nice, (and I was able to see Hammer finally see them with some Sketchfab models), but overall, I'm not really sure how much I should fret over the triangulation. Before converting a Hammer mesh to a model I bump the smoothing angle form 40 to 60 (for luck), give it a pat on the butt, and send it.

[u]Then the eureka moment finally came.[/u] I figured out you can just right click on any prop in Hammer and (without converting it to a mesh), just make a new model out of it with, generating a content dmx! JFC! It loses animation and all that stuff, but most objects are inert and this is very fast and convenient! [spoiler](Let's pray smoothing is retained tho).[/spoiler] Hell, you can do it with multiple things and make one big combination. Awkward suffixes added to the new model names hint at their provenance. '_fbx' was me using two stage Hammer, '_obj' was something that involved blender, and 'dmx' is the easy Hammer method I learned way too late.

It is important to have the object be split up into different meshes as necessary so ModelEditor can have ideal conditions to support the physics interactions you need it to (ie the multi-piece options). Please also make sure that all the pivots of all mesh components are set to the intended origin of the object. Splitting the mesh can degrade the visuals to in some instances, typically with one's own Hammer creations under bright lighting: I would have liked to add physics to the dormroom dresser and burueo models (instead of using in-map invisible Benedryl meshes), but the 'lighting seams' were too visible for my taste under those lighting conditions.

An late realization before release helped me alleviate a large portion of the startup sputter, and I also solved a mystery. I was wondering why so many of my physics props had the 'Start Asleep' flag checked, which was a setting I eschewed, and was unsure of, and infact, would do sweep after sweep in the outliner to find props that would have that flag ticked, and disable it. This was mostly a OCD thing, but I might have had a more concrete reason to not use it. I think that was because I had more props spawning 'in the air' and would certainly not want them to float when the map starts.

Turns out, the reason the props would gain that flag, was because that happens after you use the physics-diddler on them. This makes sense, as I had 'pre settled' it myself. Still, I was kinda irked that this didnt just happen at map time with it's own routine to settle everything, but I guess it doesn't really find much to do that to (it only found less than 100 objects). After realizing this, I went and settled all the props in the map myself, and the startup lag was greatly reduced.

================================================================================ [h1]INITAL SLIPSTREAM[/h1]
package.sh is a script that I run near the 'end' (ha......) of a project that gathers all assets and their dependencies from associated packs (respecting addoninfo.txt ordering) and 'slipstreams' them into a new dependency addon/asset pack for eventual release. It looks at the output of a verbose map build, which spews out just about every asset used in the map. I recently discovered that this information is also present in a nice list in a vmap text file, but I might believe that the map build log is more complete).

In byteframe13 the script was very basic: it only gathered compiled assets, and missed large swathes of content, notably skin materials. [i]It took hand holding, reruns and vconsole copy-pasta to eventually collate byteframe13.[/i] With tons of new improvements to the routine it now finds nearly everything! It still runs grep and strings against compiled asset files, so there is the potential for an occasional bork resulting in a miss, but it nails just about all of it. A proper solution would be to wrap around Source2Viewer's CLI program, but I was having trouble with running that under WSL or something.

The script can also now extract from workshop vpks (winterpeak, precious candyshop etc), -- though it doesn't decompile materials (see the above spoiler). This was added so I could slipstream that content into the project and have a smaller list of release dependencies -- for vanity purposes. I was never going to have the space, but it's a start: addingsupport later to decompile the materials of the workshop stuff at this stage would be convenient. [spoiler]There's this one wood texture in steamtours that has the most horrible reflectance.[/spoiler]

[u]More importantly, the script now finds corresponding vmats and texture files and gathers those instead of their compiled variants.[/u] Why? So that they can be edited silly! I was editing materials outside of the project addon, but it is decently cumbersome, and the asset system can only take so much tomfoolery. Plus, I wasn't going to be able to downgrade the texture resolution 'from afar' without, idk, prepping each addon with trillions of empty txt files for each texture file.

================================================================================ [h1]FILE SIZE[/h1]
I knew having a fresh site of materials I could go wild (with the actual Material Editor!) on would be important and creatively liberating, but I came at it initially from concerns about texture/file size, and some starter concerns about reflectance.

The initial size of the (compiled) slipstream materials was quite large. I forget the figure, somewhere close to 30 gigabytes or otherwise impractical to ship. A single 4k PBR material alone is monstrous, and plus theres thousands of models, with a decent likely hood of alot of new faire being at least 1024 pixels wide. I started with alot of bulk downgrades to 1/2k. It wasn't long before I become a butcher, and was also lobbing off extra stuff too: unimpressive normal maps, or those glossmaps that are just black-and-white copies of the albedo (albeit the latter is oftentimes useful as a poor-person's detail map). [spoiler]I wasn't too wary of the special textures being at a lower resolution than the color map, and now I know why materials in quest games look like they do.[/spoiler].

It was a month or two before the dust settled and it seemed like I finally cut enough weight to hit my target. At the end, I had an idea to delete all compiled materials and boot up the compiled map with the asset browser being hidden. In this way, the tools would only compile what would eventually show up in the map, and I could save space by not including things like unused skins, and what not. [b]I was hazy on the what-not, and was otherwise too timid to try.[/b] In any event, I have to ship the thing in 2GB chunks, and the inert stuff doesn't get loaded into memory, so it just occupies a portion of the 'flex' space available before the last pack hits 2 Gees.

================================================================================ [h1]PROP LOCKING[/h1]
So, while it took a few rehabs, the basic button based drawer system was quite workable. Then I noticed that the user can see and manipulate the contents of the drawer before it is opened. I never expected that NOT to be the case, but just hadn't thought of it until that point. I've played millions of VR games with cabinet props, and USUALLY they are 'locked' before the drawer opens, but I guess I never paid enough attention.

The simplest method (for me and my project) was to just set the props to 'disable pickup' and enable them when the door opens. Kinda tedious to tag all that, and only usable as a one off, but I did implement it (without testing) hoping it would be enough to not break the illusion.

Turns out (...and this maybe the source of my woes with phys/pvc attempts...) the Enable/Disable Pickup entity attributes/outputs only HALF work. They would seem to do what they say... but then, like, the vscript part of the destinations_prop still has a mind of its own and keeps doing its thing. The end result of all of this is the user CAN pick up the prop and it glitches and freezes in the air, and if its parented to something, snaps back hopefully. The only way to actually toggle pickup is with a vscript function, confusingly named 'EnableUse'. Since I can't do this in I/O (FML if its not just an undocumented FGD feature), the painful tagging of cabinet props and their doors was unworkable, and I kinda gave up.

Then I rallied and wrote some code that can find all the stuff in bounds of the child drawer prop (or a target brush) and the i/o in hammer runs the function in/out. If the drawer opens the props can be picked up, when it closes, they can't. If the dynamic container prop's origin isn't in the center, a func brush is then required. [spoiler]Was fretting real bad about the lockers not locking the props because of that.[/spoiler] It works quite nice and I think was an important win. If I had more skills (and it might not work anyway) I could connect/redirect/kajigger the i/o just right there the code instead of having to apply a moderate amount of hand holding to each drawer entity, but that can be said about alot of my scripts. [b]I still don't know how alyx or any other VR game does locks cabinet props.[/b]

================================================================================ [h1]THOUGHTS CODE VS LOGIC[/h1]
While scrolling the BlueSkee I saw Joe Wintergreen talk glowingly about IO/Entity systems like Source (he has recently implemented his own for UE5). Recently there had been the hubub around UE6 not having Blueprint. A central tenet of all the discussions is how 'writing code sucks and is hard and designers shouldn't have to do it', ergo the use of a visual or entity-based scripting system is important so people can get to work. I don't see how that's _false_, but also don't really buy the argument that writing game code is prohibitively hard for designers either. Game scripting can be a very fun and reactive endevour.

We're assuming both methods have a 1:1 set of functionality, at least for an official 'game api'. This is rarely the case, and is certainly not true for SteamVR home. [u]It's my mileu, but you gotta hod-podge it.[/u] There is much more powerful functionality in the VScript API for selecting entities, albeit that tends to be the first big hurdle in the learning curve for using the API itself.

There is no argument for having only one development method, both must coexist, it just then creates the constant question of whether something should be an entity setup or shunted to code, or work with both. The thing is, ideally everything should be script code: it's faster, there's less entities in game, you can do much more, you don't have to rebuild the map, and can iterate faster on the functionality in a more granular fashion.

I can just look at a page of code and work through the whole design, but using an entity setup, I have to slow walk the whole thing as the spaghetti-connections grow more intricate. Developing the entity systems is it's own kind of fun, and generally preferred for simple things of course. [i]They also have their own re-usability/package-ability advantages when encased in a prefab.[/i] Yet, once they get to be of sufficient complexity, it eventually becomes all but required that it all get moved over to scripts. Once you do that, then what happens inside the editor should boil down to the careful way you name all the entities (kinda like how a xml layout file is just the basic structure with a bunch of class names). I certainly can do a better job naming my entities, especially when entity setups WITH code interact (which might or might not be name-fixed up inside of a prefab). Mixed setups often result. Usually its fine, but they have somewhat of a inherent coordination challenge. I did note a couple of instances of me resorting to code to 'finish the job' when I could have just added a bit more engineering in the editor.

- The dynamic models for the toilet seats and a one or two cabinet doors (the blue wooden cabinet in east bathroom) do not glow when the user's hand moves over them. I couldn't figure out why everything else has functioning Glow Entities, and didn't want to just remake/redo them out of spite.

- The bubbler particle effect functions different on one side of the map versus the other, making half appear broken and weak. I tried to fix this. A bubbler is a water fountain.

- Similar to that weird black square in MAP01 of Doom II, there is a weird triangle artifact in the big bubbler in blue hallway. Edit: I think it's gone, the bubbler might be a dynamic now, don't recall.

- A certain amount of janky physboxes were left in for posterity or need: a few selections of vending machine content, and a cell phone. Some animated dynamic objects were also wrapped in a phys box so that they could be picked up.

- The ceiling fans in atrium are of a different ilk, and you can't shoot them. Ditto for the security cameras in library.

- The dorm light auto toggle reveals some awkwardness as it turns on even if the user explicitly turned it off prior to returning to that area, and opens the door.

================================================================================ [h1][ANNOYANCES][/h1]
- The preview window in the collision creation dialog window of Model Editor doesn't render anything.

- If editable physics collision is set to render in the Model Editor, clicking on any of the pieces in the main preview window crashes the tools. You can still manipulate the gizmos, fortunately.

- Resizing a Model Editor window with the phys collision visible is also likely to crash the tools.

- Attempting to overwrite a loaded vmap will have Hammer force the user to select a different file name, and canceling out of this crashes the tools.

- The AddAttribute/RemoveAttribute outputs either don't work, or are not detectable in vscript. (Might be PEBKAC.)

- The tools get crashy when you have a complicated quad-view setup in Hammer and do things like close maps with another one open it switches to. This _might_ only apply to closing a map with CTRL-W, whereby a click of the 'X' can be safe, and is also likely to crash if the object properties pane is smaller in the second map than in the one that was just closed.

- Seemingly due to prefab/nesting complexity, cubemaps in vrad2 bakes can become "spheres of glitch". Manually rebuilding them usually works, and (when building everything) clearing all of the lighting prior to starting vrad2 seems to give you a correct set of everything.

- Cubemaps for a vrad3 build can often times get borked, and be all red and Terminator-y. Running 'buildcubemaps' after a map of this size has fully loaded and settled corrects this.

- A static object's cubemap assignment in a vrad3 build is not always the same in vrad 2/editor. Areas with complicated overlap or priories (all these gnarly edge cases) might cause problems requiring the occasional forcing of a lighting origin, use of prop_dynamic, or more hardened bounds.

- I can't listen to DJ Stingray with BlueTooth headphones and run a Source 2 application at the same time while using Windows 10 LTSC for iOT Devices and an Intel Bluetooth driver from 2019.

- I had to use primary_black instead of tools/solidblocklight to avoid some lighting artifacts. Shucks.

- I think Valve turned off autoplay on their sharedfiles/youtube endpoint, and the internal page function doesn't start it -- one must click. Puts the kibosh on a couple of ideas I had.

- Viewing lots of particles in the asset browser window (or pane) leaks tons of VRAM, necessitating a restart of the tools.

- Soundevents of the "destinations.simple_vr_scalable" type might not make noise in NonVR mode.

- If the tools attempt to build a soundevents file which contains an empty vsnd_files array, everything crashes.

- I can't label this section 'bugs' because then it would be incumbent upon me to report them, and I am a derelict.

- If the particle editor is 'rendering sounds', they won't stop playing when you close the window, and the user must execute the 'stop_sound' console command.

- I wasn't able to use the Entity Search function to filter props based on their 'Baked Lighting' flag.

- The panorama debugger doesn't render a mouse cursor on my machine and/or is almost entirely un-intractable. Once or twice I got it to behave, and could click on stuff, barely.

- The Vconsole2.exe program inhibits display sleep on Windows. A moot point for most because they don't override steamtours.exe and vrserver.exe etc, but I do, because I like my monitor to go to sleep.  The command `powercfg /requestsoverride process vconsole2.exe display` adds it to the overrides list.
- I might have fixed an IO/Code error by putting a semicolon at the end of the RunScriptCode parameter. I was under the assumption it didn't matter, but it is more correct anyways.

- Noclipping to the exterior outside the map can hit VRAM like a ton of bricks (as one expects), but late in development seemed to crash the server/map (the tools were fine). Sn OVERFLOW error or some such was dispensed to the console.

- Using the 'Start SteamVR Workshop Tools' button in the SteamVR UI/system tray starts the tools without any proper input bindings (or whatever) and so the controllers don't work.
